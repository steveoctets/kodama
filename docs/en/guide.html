Guide

Installing

Unzip the archive, drag Kodama.app into your Applications folder, then open it. The application is signed and notarised by Apple: it starts without a warning.

Kodama does not appear in the Dock. Look for its icon in the menu bar, at the top right of the screen. On first launch, ten stations are installed.

To open the main window — the one showing the radios and the player — click the icon, then Kodama… at the bottom of the menu. Everything else is driven from that same menu; the window is only there when you need it.

Shorter still: a right-click on the icon opens the window directly, without going through the menu.

Or ⌃⌥⌘R, wherever you are. That shortcut is system-wide, unlike the menu's ⌘O, which only responds when a Kodama window is already in front — and a menu bar application almost never has focus. It can be changed in Settings, under Keyboard: if another application already uses the combination, whichever launched first keeps it.

Kodama can also launch at login: the setting is in the Startup section. It then settles into the menu bar without playing anything.

Playing a station

Click the icon, then a station. A second click stops it. The dot marks the one playing.

Your keyboard's media keys work: play/pause stops and restarts, next and previous change station — skipping to the next track would make no sense on a live stream. The order followed is the grid's, which you can rearrange by dragging the thumbnails.

Adding a radio

In the main window, the + button. Paste the stream address, or a .pls or .m3u link — Kodama follows it by itself. You can give the station a name and an image.

Searching the directory

The other path from the + button: searching radio-browser.info. Type a name, or simply pick a country and a genre to browse — that is the only way to approach fifty-eight thousand stations whose names you do not know.

Each result carries a Test button. It asks the station whether it broadcasts its titles, and so whether Kodama will be able to record it. The directory does not keep that information.

A station already in your list is flagged as such. Two mirrors of the same radio, however, remain two separate entries: nothing lets one guess they serve the same stream.

“This radio does not announce its titles”

Kodama probes the address before accepting it, and warns you if the station broadcasts no metadata. This matters: without titles, nothing says where to cut. The station is accepted if you click again, but it will only play — no track will be recorded from it.

Many general-interest radios fall into this category.

Setting the splitting

Two thresholds, in Settings:

  • Minimum duration (30 seconds by default) — below that, it is a jingle or an ad break, and the track is discarded without bothering you.
  • Maximum duration (15 minutes) — beyond that, the track is closed and a new one opens. That is what avoids an endless file on a programme that stops announcing anything.

Recording can also be turned off entirely: Kodama then just plays the radio.

And radio by radio, from each one's own sheet: do not record this radio. This catches the cases detection cannot see — a station that announces a title without ever changing it passes the test and yet splits nothing. The change takes effect on the next playback.

Deciding

Every recorded track is offered to you by a notification, with its artwork: Keep or Discard, without opening a window. If you let it pass, the track stays in the pending list.

The Recordings window shows the whole list on a large surface, grouped by station, so you can decide in batches. Several tracks can be selected at once.

Undecided tracks disappear after seven days, or when the list exceeds two gigabytes. The ones you have kept are never deleted.

Rating a track

One to five stars, while playing from the player panel, or afterwards in the Recordings window. Clicking the current star again clears the rating.

The rating is written into the file, and shows up in most players — foobar2000, MusicBee, Kodi, Plex. Apple Music ignores that field, so Kodama also writes the rating out in words in the Comments, where it stays visible.

Where the files are

In ~/Library/Application Support/Kodama/: the stations in plain text, their images, the pending list. Kept tracks go to the folder of your choice, which is configurable — changing that folder does not move what is already in it.

No index, no database: the folder is the library. Move, rename or delete in the Finder, and Kodama will read the folder again.

Taking your stations with you

The menu offers to export your stations to a .kodama file — JSON that carries the images too. On import, you choose whether to add to your list or replace it. Duplicates are discarded.

Updates

Kodama checks for new versions and offers to install them. “Check for updates…” in the menu forces an immediate check.

If something goes wrong

Reports go on GitHub. Give the version of Kodama, the version of macOS and the station concerned: many problems come from the station rather than the application, and those three lines save a round trip.
